# 2028 Prince of Wales Award application workbook

**Working draft — not an official Defence form**  
**Prepared:** 3 September 2026  
**Applicant:** [NAME]  
**Proposed activity:** [ACTIVITY AND PROVIDER]

This workbook prepares an application for the Australian Defence Force Reserves'
Prince of Wales Award (POWA). The award is made to an eligible reservist for a
future civilian professional-development activity. TacMap is evidence of the
applicant's existing performance, initiative and capacity to apply the learning;
it is not itself the award recipient.

The 2028 round has not yet been published. Based on the current annual schedule,
the likely application window is 1 July to 14 August 2027, with selection in
September–October and outcomes in November. Confirm the dates in the 2028
DEFGRAM, ForceNet notice and ADFRES application page before relying on them.

Search engines still expose an obsolete $8,000 MILPERSMAN chapter and a 2023
PH441 form with older rank and submission rules. Do not prepare or submit those
documents unless ADFRES expressly directs applicants to them for the 2028
round. The current process uses the live ADFRES online application, employer or
association input, and Commanding Officer review.

Do not enter an Employee ID, home address, protected-identity information,
medical information or other sensitive personal data in this repository. Put
those details only into the official Defence form when it opens.

## 1. Recommended application thesis

Use this as the central proposition and replace every bracketed field:

> I seek [AMOUNT] to undertake [EXACT ACTIVITY] delivered by [PROVIDER]. The
> activity will develop my capability in [SPECIFIC SKILLS] and directly benefit
> my civilian work as [ROLE] with [ORGANISATION]. I have already demonstrated my
> ability to turn professional learning into practical outcomes by creating and
> releasing TacMap, an offline-first field-navigation application for iOS and
> Android. The proposed activity addresses the identified gap in [GAP], which I
> cannot reasonably access through existing employer or personal resources. I
> will transfer the learning through [DELIVERABLES], measured by [KPIs], for the
> benefit of [ORGANISATION AND ITS USERS/CLIENTS]. Funding is sought for
> professional development, not product development.

The application should remain civilian-first. Reserve experience may explain
the applicant's perspective and commitment, but the scored benefit must be real
and direct for the civilian occupation or organisation.

## 3. Information needed to personalise the draft

Provide these facts without sensitive identifiers:

1. Service, substantive rank and SERCAT.
2. RSD totals for FY2024–25 and FY2025–26, plus the FY2026–27 plan.
3. Civilian employment: employer and role, or the nature of the self-employed business.
4. How TacMap forms part of, or benefits, that civilian occupation.
5. The professional capability you most need to develop.
6. Preferred activity type: qualification/course, conference/symposium, or a coherent combination to be confirmed with ADFRES.
7. Whether an employer, professional association and CO are already supportive.
8. Any previous POWA and year.

## 4. Selection-criteria map

| Criterion | Weight | Required argument | Best evidence |
|---|---:|---|---|
| Relevance | 10 | The activity develops the applicant professionally and creates clear, tangible civilian benefits | Skills-gap analysis, provider syllabus, measurable deliverables, employer confirmation |
| Opportunity | 5 | POWA makes possible a unique or otherwise unattainable opportunity | Cost and access barrier, comparison with alternatives, explanation of why normal funding is unavailable |
| Civilian support | 5 | The applicant performs strongly and contributes materially in the civilian workplace | Specific employer/association statement with examples and expected benefit |
| Application quality | 5 | The proposal is clear, complete, persuasive and financially sound | Plain-English structure, exact dates, itemised quote, co-funding explanation and realistic measures |
| CO endorsement | Yes/No | The member is eligible, suitable and supportable | Early CO brief, verified service record and an OPSEC-safe representation plan |

There are no separate points for the number of TacMap features, code volume or
military relevance. Product evidence earns its place only when it proves
professional contribution, establishes the baseline for future benefit, or
makes the proposed development need credible.

## 5. Select the professional-development activity

The final application needs an exact activity rather than a general wish to
develop TacMap. Plausible themes include:

- advanced geospatial engineering, cartography or spatial-data assurance;
- secure mobile application engineering or software assurance;
- human factors, accessibility and evaluation of field systems;
- emergency-management or field-GIS interoperability; or
- digital product leadership and commercialisation, if that is the genuine
  civilian need.

## 8. Suggested unclassified field-trial protocol

Use only approved people, places, map data and scenarios. Obtain consent before
collecting quotations or participant-level data.

### Purpose

Determine whether TacMap improves selected civilian field-navigation tasks over
the current comparison workflow, and identify limitations requiring
professional development.

### Candidate tasks

1. Import and open a supplied offline map.
2. Navigate to a supplied MGRS or latitude/longitude reference.
3. Calibrate a non-georeferenced map and record residual and ground error.
4. Create, edit and export a waypoint, line and area.
5. Round-trip exported data through a named GIS tool.
6. Complete a defined workflow with all non-essential network paths disabled.
7. Recover safely from interruption, malformed input or application restart.

### Measures

- task-success rate;
- completion time versus the comparison method;
- coordinate/calibration error against known truth;
- import/export data loss or semantic change;
- user-reported workload and usability;
- crashes, hangs, data-loss events and recovery time;
- battery consumption over a defined period; and
- observed network connections under the stated configuration.

Define thresholds before testing. Record failures and limitations rather than
selecting only favourable examples.

## 14. Claims and OPSEC rules

A safe baseline description is:

> TacMap is an independently developed civilian field-mapping application
> evaluated with synthetic and public-domain data. No ADF endorsement,
> accreditation, procurement, adoption or operational use is claimed.

- Use only unclassified, authorised scenarios, maps, screenshots and quotations.
- Do not identify operational locations, sensitive workflows or protected people.
- Do not claim that TacMap is adopted, approved, endorsed or accredited by
  Defence unless documentary authority supports those exact words.
- Avoid absolute terms such as “military-grade”, “fully secure”, “zero data
  collection” or “works everywhere” unless independently verified and carefully
  scoped.
- Say which release and configuration was tested.
- Distinguish application-level traffic from operating-system, app-store and
  third-party-provider traffic.
- Obtain written permission for employer, unit, organisation and individual names,
  logos, quotations, photographs and trial results.
- Raise protected-identity and public-representation constraints privately with
  the CO and ADFRES early.
